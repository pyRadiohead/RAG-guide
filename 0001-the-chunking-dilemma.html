Lesson 01 · Chunking for RAG

The Chunking Dilemma

Why there is no "best" chunk size — only a tradeoff you steer.

Your mission: pick a chunking strategy for an upcoming project and defend it. You can't defend a choice until you know the axis every choice trades on. That axis is this lesson.

1The one tension

Retrieval returns chunks, not documents. So how you cut the text decides what the LLM can ever see. Make the cuts small and you get sharp, on-target hits — but you slice ideas apart and lose context. Make them big and each chunk carries full context — but every hit drags in paragraphs of irrelevant noise.

That is the whole game. Two poles, pulling opposite ways:

↓ Small chunks

  • High precision — little noise per hit
  • Tight, specific matches
  • But: ideas get split across boundaries
  • But: a chunk can lose its own context ("revenue grew 3%" — whose?)

↑ Large chunks

  • High recall — sweeps in the answer
  • Full context travels with the text
  • But: noise dilutes the context window
  • But: embeddings get "blurry" — many topics, one vector

Precision = of what you returned, how much was actually relevant. Recall = of what was relevant, how much you returned. Chunk size pushes them in opposite directions — you don't get both for free. (Full definitions in your glossary.)
